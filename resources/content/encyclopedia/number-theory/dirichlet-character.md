## Overview

Dirichlet characters convert multiplicative residue-class information into complex-valued functions. They are the finite-group analogue of Fourier characters and provide a bridge from congruences to analytic number theory. Their values are constrained by multiplication, not assigned freely to each residue. The associated L-series organizes these periodic arithmetic values into an analytic function.

## Prerequisites

Know the unit group modulo an integer, group homomorphisms, complex roots of unity and convergent series. A group character in this article is one-dimensional and complex-valued, not a trace character of an arbitrary higher-dimensional representation.

## Definition and conventions

Fix $N\ge2$. A Dirichlet character modulo $N$ is a homomorphism

$$
\chi:(\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z)^\times\longrightarrow\mathbb C^\times.
$$

Extend it to all integers by periodicity on units and by $\chi(n)=0$ when $\gcd(n,N)>1$. Its nonzero values are roots of unity because the domain is finite. The extended function is completely multiplicative: $\chi(ab)=\chi(a)\chi(b)$ for all integers. The principal character has value one on every unit and zero on every nonunit.

The conductor is the least positive divisor $f$ of $N$ through whose unit group the character factors. A character is primitive when $f=N$. Factoring here refers to its values on units; extensions by zero for different moduli need not coincide on all integers.

## Intuition

Characters are multiplicative probes of a finite group. Their complex phases distinguish residues while keeping multiplication simple. Zero extension excludes residues outside the unit group. Confusing the principal character with the constant-one function loses that exclusion and changes its L-function.

## Examples and non-examples

Modulo $5$, the unit group is generated by $2$. Choosing $\chi(2)=i$ forces

$$
\chi(1)=1,\quad\chi(2)=i,\quad\chi(4)=-1,\quad\chi(3)=-i.
$$

Every multiple of $5$ has value zero. For example, $\chi(6)\chi(7)=1\cdot i=\chi(42)$. In contrast, declaring the value of every unit to be $-1$ fails at the identity, which any homomorphism must send to one. A general periodic function need not be a character.

## Important properties

For a nonprincipal character, the sum over the unit group is zero. Choose a unit $u$ with $\chi(u)\ne1$. Multiplication by $u$ permutes the group, so the sum $S$ satisfies $S=\chi(u)S$, forcing $S=0$. Applying this to the quotient of two distinct characters gives their orthogonality.

For $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, the series $L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n\ge1}\chi(n)n^{-s}$ converges absolutely. Complete multiplicativity and prime factorization give an Euler product. For the principal character modulo $N$, the product omits the primes dividing $N$, so it is not simply the Riemann zeta function without those factors being removed.

## Connections and further reading

Character sums isolate arithmetic progressions, and primitive characters determine cleaner functional equations. Characters also appear in modular-form transformation laws.
