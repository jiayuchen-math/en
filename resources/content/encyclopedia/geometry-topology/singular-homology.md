## Overview

Singular homology associates abelian groups to a space by comparing cycles with boundaries. It can be defined on every topological space without choosing a triangulation. The construction uses many parametrized simplices, often infinitely many possible generators, but each individual chain remains a finite formal sum. Smaller models are used for calculations when justified.

## Prerequisites

Understand free abelian groups, kernels, images, and quotient groups. A chain complex is a sequence of groups and boundary maps whose consecutive composites vanish. Geometric pictures help, but a cycle is an algebraic condition on a formal sum, not necessarily one embedded closed surface.

## Definition and conventions

The standard simplex $\Delta^n$ has ordered vertices $v_0,\ldots,v_n$. A singular simplex is a continuous map $\sigma:\Delta^n\to X$. With integral coefficients, $C_n(X)$ is the free abelian group generated by these maps. Its boundary is
$$
\partial\sigma=\sum_{i=0}^n(-1)^i\sigma|_{[v_0,\ldots,\widehat v_i,\ldots,v_n]}.
$$
The face restrictions use the induced vertex order. Set $C_n=0$ for negative degrees and define $H_n(X;\mathbb Z)=\ker\partial_n/\operatorname{im}\partial_{n+1}$.

This entry uses ordinary, unreduced homology. Other coefficient groups and reduced homology must be stated explicitly. Hausdorff and countability conditions are unnecessary for the construction.

## Intuition

A cycle has no remaining boundary after cancellation. A boundary already arises as the edge of a higher-dimensional chain, so it contributes no new homology class. A nonzero class is an obstruction to filling a cycle within the space. Different chains represent the same class when their difference can be filled.

## Examples and non-examples

For a point, every positive-dimensional homology group vanishes and $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$. The circle has $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$ and $H_1\cong\mathbb Z$, with higher groups zero. A single traversal represents a generator in degree one.

For the disk, the boundary-circle cycle becomes a boundary and vanishes in $H_1(D^2)$. It remains nonzero when considered in the circle itself. Thus the same visible curve may be trivial or nontrivial according to the ambient space. For a space with two path components, $H_0$ is the direct sum of two copies of $\mathbb Z$.

## Important properties

Every codimension-two face appears twice in $\partial^2\sigma$, with opposite signs, proving $\partial^2=0$. Continuous maps induce chain maps by composition and hence homology homomorphisms.

Homotopic maps induce the same homomorphism. Triangulating a simplex times an interval gives a prism operator whose boundary identity is the required chain homotopy. Consequently contractible nonempty spaces have the homology of a point. Homology alone does not classify homotopy types.

## Connections and further reading

Cellular homology computes the same invariant on CW complexes using their cells. Cohomology reverses the direction of induced maps and adds products. Read [Hatcher, §§2.1–2.2](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf) for the inspected definition, signs, and comparison.
