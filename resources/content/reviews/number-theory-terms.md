## Mathematical review and conventions

Definitions specify positive primes, positive congruence moduli, the coprimality hypotheses of Euler and Fermat, pairwise coprimality for the integer CRT, primitive roots of unity for cyclotomic polynomials, and monic equations for algebraic integers. The Pell entry selects right side +1 and acknowledges the broader ±1 convention. An integral basis is a Z-basis of the full integer ring, not merely a rational vector-space basis. The trace and norm entries fix the ambient extension K/Q. The norm example is negative, which prevents confusion with metric or ideal norms.

Local e/f, unramified and tame entries are explicitly restricted to p-adic local fields with finite residue fields. This makes residue separability automatic without asserting that e=1 alone suffices over every discretely valued field. The chosen Chinese headword 惯性指数 is Zhang's actual name for residue degree, not a claim that it means the order of an inertia group. The Hilbert class field requires real places to split. Adeles require integrality at almost every finite place, whereas ideles require units there.

Characters include the zero extension at nonunits; the conductor is distinguished from the displayed modulus. A nonprimitive character can differ from its primitive inducing character at removed Euler factors. Gauss sums are finite sums, but their square-root magnitude assertion is confined to primitive characters. L-series definitions state their convergence half-plane and distinguish that series from continuation.

Modular forms are holomorphic at every cusp; cusp forms have zero constant term at every cusp; modular functions may have cusp poles. The Eisenstein entry fixes even weight at least 4 and normalized constant term 1. The Hecke entry fixes full level and arithmetic normalization, so no bad-prime higher-level formula is silently substituted. Newform means normalized Hecke eigenvector in the Petersson new subspace, not simply any vector in that subspace.

Examples were checked directly: totient(18)=6; the CRT example is 8 modulo 15; the Hensel lift satisfies 10 squared minus 2 = 98 modulo 49; the quadratic trace, norm and discriminant examples give 6, -1 and 5; X squared plus 1 is irreducible over F_3, giving the unramified quadratic example; the modulo-3 Gauss sum is i times sqrt(3); T_2 acts on E_4 by 9 and on normalized Delta by -24. No source prose or textbook exercise was copied.
