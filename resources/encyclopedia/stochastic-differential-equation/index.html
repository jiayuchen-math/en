Overview

A stochastic differential equation, or SDE, couples an evolving state to a random driver. Its differential notation abbreviates an integral identity. At research level, specifying the noise, integration convention, admissible solutions, and uniqueness notion is essential: a formal expression alone does not determine a well-posed problem.

This overview is scoped to finite-dimensional equations driven by Brownian motion. Jump-driven equations, distributional coefficients, and stochastic partial differential equations require additional frameworks and are not covered by the existence statement below.

Prerequisites

The classical formulation uses a filtered probability space, an adapted Brownian motion, and Itô integration. Useful analytic tools include moment estimates, Lipschitz continuity, and Gronwall's inequality. Pathwise approaches also study iterated integrals of the driver, rather than only its time-indexed values.

A strong solution uses the prescribed noise and initial variable. A weak solution allows the probability space and driver to be part of the construction. These uses of “strong” and “weak” concern solutions, not convergence modes or mixing.

Intuition

Over a small interval, the drift contributes at scale hhh and the noise at scale h\sqrt hh​. The state's influence on the noise amplitude creates the quadratic correction in Itô's change-of-variable formula. Approximating a rough driver by smooth paths can change the limiting equation unless the integration convention is accounted for.

Important properties

If bbb and σ\sigmaσ are globally Lipschitz, satisfy linear growth, and the initial variable is square-integrable and independent of future Brownian increments, the classical equation has a global strong solution with pathwise uniqueness. Picard iteration supplies a proof idea: Itô isometry controls successive stochastic-integral differences, while deterministic integral estimates and Gronwall control their accumulation.

Local Lipschitz conditions generally supply a solution only up to explosion. Merely continuous coefficients do not justify asserting the same uniqueness theorem.

For a scalar equation with differentiable σ\sigmaσ, a Stratonovich diffusion term σ(Xt)∘dBt\sigma(X_t)\circ dB_tσ(Xt​)∘dBt​ corresponds to an Itô drift correction σσ′/2\sigma\sigma'/2σσ′/2. The integration symbol and correction must therefore be specified together.

Connections and further reading

Research questions include stability of solutions under noise approximation and formulations with less regular coefficients. Rough paths encode iterated increments to make appropriate solution maps continuous; this is a distinct mathematical mechanism, not ordinary differentiation of the noise. Lawler, §§3.2–3.5 develops the integral and classical estimates. Friz–Hairer, Chapter 9, Theorems 9.1 and 9.3 connects rough equations, SDEs, and smooth approximation.
