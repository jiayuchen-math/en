覆盖空间与伽罗瓦理论：比较两个对应

中文导读 · 非逐句全文翻译 · 修订 2026-10-17

这两个方向的联系不只是“都研究对称性”。更适合动手比较的是子群对应：对象怎样对应到子群，包含关系朝哪个方向走，正规性又在哪里出现。

拓扑一侧先限于连通、局部道路连通、半局部单连通的空间，并选择基点。在这个经典设置下，连通带基点覆盖对应于基本群的子群；忘掉基点后对应的是子群的共轭类。圆周的 z↦znz\mapsto z^nz↦zn 对应整数群中的 nZn\mathbb ZnZ，覆盖层数就是子群指数。不要把这个分类不加条件地推广到任意空间。

域论一侧，固定有限伽罗瓦扩张 L/KL/KL/K。中间域与伽罗瓦群的子群构成反向包含对应。可以用 Q(2,3)/Q\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)/\mathbb QQ(2​,3​)/Q 算出三个二次中间域，再核对扩张次数与子群指数。完整对应的有限伽罗瓦假设不能删掉。

最后专门比较正规性：在上述拓扑设置中，连通覆盖是正规覆盖当且仅当对应子群正规；一般的甲板变换群是正规化子商，而不总是整个基本群的商。域论中也要以正规子群为条件，才能把相应商群解释为中间扩张的伽罗瓦群。圆周基本群是交换群，反而会掩盖这层区别。

Connecting ideas

Compare classical connected covering classification with a finite Galois correspondence, including basepoints, inclusion reversal, and normal subgroups.

The productive connection is a pair of subgroup correspondences. Work through one example on each side, then compare the directions of the maps and the role of normality. The word “symmetry” alone is too loose to guide a calculation.

On the topological side, restrict the base XXX to a connected, locally path-connected, semilocally simply-connected space. Connectedness together with local path-connectedness makes XXX path-connected. Choose x0∈Xx_0\in Xx0​∈X. These are the hypotheses for the classical classification used here; this guide makes no classification claim for arbitrary spaces.

Start with the circle

For an integer n≥1n\geq1n≥1, pn:S1→S1p_n:S^1\to S^1pn​:S1→S1, z↦znz\mapsto z^nz↦zn, is a connected nnn-sheeted covering. With both basepoints equal to 111, its induced subgroup in π1(S1,1)≅Z\pi_1(S^1,1)\cong\mathbb Zπ1​(S1,1)≅Z is nZn\mathbb ZnZ. A loop of winding number kkk lifts to a closed loop precisely when nnn divides kkk. Its deck transformations multiply zzz by an nnnth root of unity.

Allen Hatcher's Algebraic Topology, §1.3, Theorem 1.38 classifies connected pointed covers by subgroups H=p∗π1(Y,y0)H=p_*\pi_1(Y,y_0)H=p∗​π1​(Y,y0​) of π1(X,x0)\pi_1(X,x_0)π1​(X,x0​). Forgetting the chosen point replaces subgroups by conjugacy classes. The number of sheets is the subgroup index, which may be infinite.

In a fixed universal cover, write the corresponding cover as XH=X~/HX_H=\widetilde X/HXH​=X/H, using a consistent deck-action convention. Inclusion H⊆KH\subseteq KH⊆K gives a covering map XH→XKX_H\to X_KXH​→XK​ over XXX. A larger subgroup gives a further quotient and fewer sheets when the indices are finite. For example, 6Z⊆2Z6\mathbb Z\subseteq2\mathbb Z6Z⊆2Z gives a three-sheeted map between the six-sheeted and two-sheeted circle covers.

Put a finite field extension beside it

Let L/KL/KL/K be a finite Galois extension and G=Gal⁡(L/K)G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)G=Gal(L/K). Intermediate fields K⊆E⊆LK\subseteq E\subseteq LK⊆E⊆L correspond to subgroups via E↦Gal⁡(L/E)E\mapsto\operatorname{Gal}(L/E)E↦Gal(L/E) and H↦LHH\mapsto L^HH↦LH. This correspondence reverses inclusion, and [LH:K]=[G:H][L^H:K]=[G:H][LH:K]=[G:H]. The hypotheses belong to the theorem: do not apply this full correspondence to an arbitrary finite extension.

Read J. S. Milne's Fields and Galois Theory, Chapter 3, Theorem 3.17. Then calculate with L=Q(2,3)L=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)L=Q(2​,3​) over K=QK=\mathbb QK=Q. Independent sign changes give a group of order four. Fixing 2\sqrt22​ leaves a subgroup of order two; its fixed field is Q(2)\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)Q(2​). Find the other two quadratic intermediate fields and check each degree against its subgroup index.

Normality is the crucial checkpoint

A connected cover in this setting is regular, also called normal or Galois, exactly when HHH is normal in π1(X,x0)\pi_1(X,x_0)π1​(X,x0​). Then its deck group is π1(X,x0)/H\pi_1(X,x_0)/Hπ1​(X,x0​)/H. In general the deck group is N(H)/HN(H)/HN(H)/H, with N(H)N(H)N(H) the normalizer; see Hatcher, Proposition 1.39. Connected covers need not be Galois. Circle covers hide this distinction because every subgroup of Z\mathbb ZZ is normal.

On the field side, LH/KL^H/KLH/K is Galois exactly when HHH is normal in GGG, and then its Galois group is G/HG/HG/H. Draw the subgroup and intermediate-field diagrams for the biquadratic example, reversing the inclusions. Afterwards compare the circle diagram. Keep both ambient objects visible: the universal cover on one side and the chosen finite Galois extension on the other. Their shared pattern is useful without identifying topological spaces with fields or assuming every intermediate object has a quotient group of symmetries.
