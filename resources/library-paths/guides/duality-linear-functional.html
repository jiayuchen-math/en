从线性泛函到分析中的对偶

中文导读 · 非逐句全文翻译 · 修订 2026-10-17

“对偶”不是脱离结构的一个统一对象。先说明标量域，以及是否要求连续性。向量空间的代数对偶包含所有线性泛函；赋范空间的连续对偶只保留连续线性泛函。

有限维时，取值给出自然映射 J:V→V∗∗J:V\to V^{**}J:V→V∗∗，即 J(v)(φ)=φ(v)J(v)(\varphi)=\varphi(v)J(v)(φ)=φ(v)，它是同构。这个定义不依赖基；而把 VVV 与 V∗V^*V∗ 认同，通常需要额外选择或结构。对线性映射做复合还会反转方向，产生 T∗:W∗→V∗T^*:W^*\to V^*T∗:W∗→V∗，可以用一个小矩阵核对转置出现的原因。

进入分析后，范数开始决定哪些线性测量是稳定的。在 [0,1][0,1][0,1] 上的实多项式空间取上确界范数，p↦p′(0)p\mapsto p'(0)p↦p′(0) 虽然线性却不连续：pn(t)=(1−t)np_n(t)=(1-t)^npn​(t)=(1−t)n 的范数恒为 111，导数在零点的绝对值却是 nnn。有限维中“线性就连续”的捷径不能照搬。

对连续对偶再次取连续对偶，Hahn–Banach 定理给出自然的等距嵌入。Banach 空间的自反性要求这个特定嵌入满射。例子 c0c_0c0​ 的连续对偶为 ℓ1\ell^1ℓ1，连续双对偶为 ℓ∞\ell^\inftyℓ∞，常数 111 序列不在自然嵌入的像里。阅读时标出有限维论证用到了维数的地方，再看无限维版本如何改用 Hahn–Banach。

Connecting ideas

Follow evaluation from finite-dimensional algebraic biduality to normed-space continuous duals, with an unbounded functional and a nonreflexive Banach space.

A linear functional turns a vector into a scalar. Coordinate extraction, integration against a fixed function, and point evaluation can all fit this description, but the meaning of “dual” depends on what structure the functional must respect. Begin by declaring the scalar field and whether topology is part of the problem.

For a vector space VVV over a field kkk, its algebraic dual is V∗=Hom⁡k(V,k)V^*=\operatorname{Hom}_k(V,k)V∗=Homk​(V,k): all linear functionals, with no continuity condition. On k2k^2k2, each functional has the form φ(x,y)=ax+by\varphi(x,y)=ax+byφ(x,y)=ax+by. A chosen basis supplies the coefficients; the functional itself exists independently of that choice.

Follow the evaluation map

There is a canonical linear map

J:V⟶V∗∗,J(v)(φ)=φ(v).J:V\longrightarrow V^{**},\qquad J(v)(\varphi)=\varphi(v).J:V⟶V∗∗,J(v)(φ)=φ(v).

If VVV is finite-dimensional, JJJ is an isomorphism. To check injectivity, a nonzero vector can be included in a basis and detected by a coordinate functional. Dimension then gives surjectivity. This identification with the bidual does not require a basis choice in its definition. An identification V≅V∗V\cong V^*V≅V∗ generally does require extra choices or structure.

Read Sheldon Axler's Linear Algebra Done Right, §3F, and then Exercise 32 on printed page 118, which asks for the evaluation construction. The prime notation for the dual in that book differs from the star notation used here. Translate the notation before comparing formulas.

For a linear map T:V→WT:V\to WT:V→W, composing functionals with TTT gives T∗:W∗→V∗T^*:W^*\to V^*T∗:W∗→V∗. This reversal of direction is worth checking on a small matrix. It explains why a transpose appears in dual coordinates without introducing an inner product or an adjoint.

Add a norm and ask which measurements are stable

Now let XXX be a normed space over R\mathbb RR or C\mathbb CC, with its norm topology. Write X′X'X′ for its continuous dual. A linear functional belongs to X′X'X′ exactly when ∣φ(x)∣≤C∥x∥|\varphi(x)|\leq C\|x\|∣φ(x)∣≤C∥x∥ for some finite constant CCC. Equip X′X'X′ with the operator norm. All linear functionals on a finite-dimensional normed space are continuous, but that shortcut fails in infinite dimensions.

For example, give the real polynomial space on [0,1][0,1][0,1] the supremum norm. The functional D(p)=p′(0)D(p)=p'(0)D(p)=p′(0) is linear but not continuous: for pn(t)=(1−t)np_n(t)=(1-t)^npn​(t)=(1−t)n, the norms equal 111 while ∣D(pn)∣=n|D(p_n)|=n∣D(pn​)∣=n. Changing the norm can change which functionals are continuous. A claim about “the dual” of a function space is incomplete until that structure is specified.

With the continuous dual used at both stages, evaluation defines J:X→X′′J:X\to X''J:X→X′′. Hahn–Banach shows that it is an isometric embedding. A Banach space is reflexive when this particular map is onto. Infinite dimension alone does not decide reflexivity, and an abstract isomorphism with some other space is not the definition.

Richard Melrose's MIT notes, Chapter 1, §§12–13 give the normed-space argument. Their example c0c_0c0​, the scalar sequences tending to zero with the supremum norm, has continuous dual ℓ1\ell^1ℓ1 and continuous bidual ℓ∞\ell^\inftyℓ∞. Under these identifications, JJJ is the usual inclusion; the constant sequence 111 witnesses failure of surjectivity. These statements concern continuous duals, not algebraic duals.

As a reading exercise, put the finite-dimensional and normed-space arguments side by side. Mark exactly where dimension was used in the first and Hahn–Banach in the second. Keeping those reasons visible prevents a familiar finite-dimensional conclusion from silently becoming an unjustified infinite-dimensional claim.
