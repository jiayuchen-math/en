Meaning in context

An isolated singularity a of f is a pole of order m>=1 if f(z)=g(z)/(z-a)^m near a with g holomorphic and g(a) nonzero.

孤立奇点 a 附近若 f 可写为全纯函数 g 除以 (z-a)^m，且 g(a) 不为零、m 为正整数，则 a 是 m 阶极点。

Example

f(z)=(z+3)/(z−1)2f(z)=(z+3)/(z-1)^2f(z)=(z+3)/(z−1)2 has a pole of order 222 at 111 since the numerator there is 444.

Usage and distinctions

At a pole the Laurent principal part is finite and nonempty; an essential singularity has infinitely many negative terms.
