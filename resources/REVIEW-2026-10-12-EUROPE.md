## Evidence and limits

Current official rosters were read on October 12. French professors, maîtres de conférences and permanent research director/research officer categories were retained; doctoral students, postdocs, visitors and explicitly emeritus/retired entries were excluded. Italian contract teaching, unrelated departments and unverified individual eligibility checks were excluded. A permanent rank does not establish HDR, doctoral-supervision eligibility, available places or a guaranteed degree route.

Orsay and I2M group labels are marked **group membership**, not personal research-interest statements. Their original French labels and English search translations are retained. Shared laboratory affiliations do not identify every person's legal employer. Public emails were taken from literal official contacts or transparent published “at/dot” encodings, never guessed from names. Bonn roster email conflicts for Thibault Décoppet and Jochen Garcke were withheld. Unknown personal contacts/topics remain explicitly unknown.

The combined directory has 952 personal published-topic records, 324 group-membership records and 663 unknown-topic records. These disjoint counts sum to 1,939. The three existing public student-completion records retain their original dated evidence; no new current vacancy is asserted.

## Country gap audit

`study-careers/coverage.html` and `data/researcher-coverage.json` list an 80-country/territory checklist: continental Europe, existing programme destinations and additional regional priorities. It is **not every country in the world**, a faculty census, a ranking or an admissions predictor. The region label uses the directory's geographic grouping, not a political-membership test. Country refers to indexed affiliation location, not researcher nationality.

Every row includes indexed people, institution labels/units, sourced contacts, topic-evidence gaps, source URLs and existing programme-route counts. Zero countries remain visible. Status is reproducible: zero = unrepresented, 1–5 = very limited sample, larger counts = single- or multi-institution sample. No country is labelled complete; institution labels are not asserted as unique universities.

Remaining major European gaps include Poland, Finland, Norway, Greece, Hungary, Ireland, Romania and other Baltic/Balkan/eastern-European destinations. Several new countries remain single-school samples. Elsewhere, India, Israel, South Africa and Uruguay still have only one indexed researcher each; major Latin American and other destinations remain unrepresented. This audit is a backlog, not a claim those countries lack mathematicians.
