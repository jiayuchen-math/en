Abstract Algebra & Galois Theory

抽象代数：为什么要把对象取商？

独立中文导读 · 非逐句全文翻译 · 修订 2026-10-18

模 666 同余把无穷多个整数装进六个类中，而加法与乘法仍然说得通。“把东西合并以后，运算为什么还成立？”是进入抽象代数很好的问题。不是每一种划分类都允许你这样做，这正是正规子群和理想会出现的原因。

让商对象先有一个用途

推荐 Judson 的 Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications。若群的定义与陪集已熟，先读第 10 章正规子群与商群、第 11 章同态，再在第 16 章比较理想和商环。读一个同构定理时，把映射、核、像与诱导映射写全；“两边看起来一样大”不足以建立同构。

在非交换群中，试着用一个不正规的子群定义陪集乘法，观察它为什么可能依赖代表元。在环中则计算模 666 的 2⋅32\cdot32⋅3，分清“非零”与“可逆”。这些小例子能阻止你把域或向量空间里习惯的结论直接搬到一般环。

再选群作用或域的路线

若目标是伽罗瓦理论，接第 21 章域以及 23.1–23.2 的自同构与基本定理。先会多项式与域扩张的计算，再画中间域与子群的反向包含图。完整对应的有限伽罗瓦条件不能删掉。若目标是表示论，可以先把群作用在集合和向量空间上的例子做熟，不必把全书所有专题依次读完。

自检时，证明多项式在 aaa 处取值的映射之核是理想 (x−a)(x-a)(x−a)，再解释相应商环；另取一个小有限群，列出子群并检查哪些正规。能背“核一定正规”不等于能检查一个具体子群。主读物有阅读问题与习题，公开完整解答没有核实，适合先自行写出计算或证明再与页面自测对照。

已有商与同态经验的读者可略读定义复习。之后交换代数会研究局部化、整性与模，表示论会把群变成线性变换；它们各自需要不同的计算，不是抽象程度越高就越可以省掉例子。

Studying Abstract Algebra & Galois Theory

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Quotients are an organizing construction in algebra: they identify elements while preserving the operations that matter. Judson provides a broad route through groups, rings and fields with reading questions and exercises. Milne's field notes narrow the focus to extensions and Galois theory. Start with the broad text if well-defined maps or quotient rings remain uncertain; use the field route when you can already move between subgroups and homomorphisms.

What to work on

  • Construct quotient groups and use the first isomorphism theorem.
  • Distinguish prime and maximal ideals through quotient rings.
  • Compute degrees and bases of simple field extensions.
  • Match subgroups and intermediate fields in a finite Galois extension.

A starting text

Abstract Algebra: Theory and Applications — Thomas W. Judson; Sage exercises by Robert A. Beezer. For quotients read chapters 10, Normal Subgroups and Factor Groups, and 11, Homomorphisms. Review the ideal and quotient-ring material in chapter 16, Rings. For the field route use chapter 21, Fields, then sections 23.1-23.2 on field automorphisms and the fundamental theorem.

Choosing what to read

Build a quotient map and prove that its induced map is well-defined before using an isomorphism theorem. Compare ideals through their quotient rings, then compute a small field extension with an explicit basis. For the biquadratic extension of the rationals, draw the subgroup and intermediate-field correspondences and check each fixed field by hand. Separate normality and separability in the theorem's hypotheses, giving an example of what each condition controls.

Try these before moving on

Try a proof or calculation before opening the hint. These solutions address the checks below, not the exercises in the linked textbooks.

  1. List the ideals of Z/12Z and identify which are prime or maximal by examining their quotient rings.

    A hint

    Pull ideals back to ideals of the integers containing (12). Include the zero ideal and the whole ring, but exclude the whole ring when testing primality.

    Work through the solution

    Write R=Z/12ZR=\mathbb Z/12\mathbb ZR=Z/12Z. By inverse image under the quotient map, its ideals correspond to (d)⊆Z(d)\subseteq\mathbb Z(d)⊆Z with (12)⊆(d)(12)\subseteq(d)(12)⊆(d), equivalently positive divisors ddd of 121212. They are (1ˉ),(2ˉ),(3ˉ),(4ˉ),(6ˉ),(12ˉ)(\bar1), (\bar2), (\bar3), (\bar4), (\bar6), (\bar{12})(1ˉ),(2ˉ),(3ˉ),(4ˉ),(6ˉ),(12ˉ), the last being the zero ideal. For each such ddd, R/(dˉ)≅Z/dZR/(\bar d)\cong\mathbb Z/d\mathbb ZR/(dˉ)≅Z/dZ.

    For d=2,3d=2,3d=2,3 this quotient is a field, hence a domain, so these ideals are both maximal and prime. For d=4,6,12d=4,6,12d=4,6,12 it has nonzero zero divisors: respectively 2⋅22\cdot22⋅2, 2⋅32\cdot32⋅3, and 3⋅43\cdot43⋅4 vanish modulo ddd. Those ideals are neither prime nor maximal. The case d=1d=1d=1 is the whole ring, excluded by the requirement that prime and maximal ideals be proper. This exhausts all ideals.

    If this was the sticking point:

    • Elementary Number Theory — Review divisibility in the integers and arithmetic in residue rings.
    • Proofs & Mathematical Writing — Practice proving a list is exhaustive using a correspondence rather than examples alone.

Already comfortable with this? Skip quotient review if you can construct the first-isomorphism map and prove it is well-defined. Skip the field route if you can complete the biquadratic Galois correspondence and explain why separability and normality enter the general theorem.
