Studying Commutative Algebra

What to work on

  • Test prime and maximal ideals using quotients.
  • Construct a localization and track extension and contraction of ideals.
  • Use the ascending-chain condition and Hilbert's basis theorem.
  • Prove that a specified element is integral by finding a monic equation.

A starting text

A Primer of Commutative Algebra — James S. Milne. Read section 2, Ideals, as needed; then section 3, Noetherian rings, printed pages 9-13; section 5, Rings of fractions, pages 18-24; and section 6, Integral dependence, pages 25-29. Use section 14, The spectrum of a ring, only for the geometric continuation.

Practice

Localization and integrality exercises with selected solutions. Exercises in sections 2, 3, 5 and 6; consult Appendix A only for exercise numbers actually listed there.. Exercises provided; public solutions to selected numbered exercises are present in Appendix A, printed page 110. Full coverage is not claimed.

Try these before moving on

  • Describe Z localized at the complement of (5), determine its units, and list its prime ideals with justification.
  • For a field k, prove k[t] is integral over k[t squared, t cubed] and identify an element of its fraction field integral over the smaller ring but absent from it.

Already comfortable with this? Skip sections 2-3 if ideal correspondences and Noetherian proofs are routine. Skip localization and integrality only if you can do both checks and state the universal property of localization; familiarity with the notation S inverse A is insufficient.
