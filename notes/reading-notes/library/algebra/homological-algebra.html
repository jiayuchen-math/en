Studying Homological Algebra

What to work on

  • Compute homology from kernels and images in an explicit complex.
  • Construct a connecting map for a short exact sequence of complexes.
  • Build a free resolution of a cyclic module.
  • Compute first Ext and Tor groups from that resolution.

A starting text

Notes on Basic Homological Algebra — Andrew Baker. Read sections 1-3: Chain complexes and their homology; Short exact sequences of chain complexes; Chain homotopies and quasi-isomorphisms. In section 2 work through the connecting-map construction before proceeding to resolutions in the alternative text.

Other ways into the subject
  • Introduction to Representation Theory — Pavel Etingof, Oleg Golberg, Sebastian Hensel, Tiankai Liu, Alex Schwendner, Dmitry Vaintrob and Elena Yudovina. Sections 7.8-7.9 on complexes and exact functors; 8.1-8.2 on projective modules, resolutions, Tor and Ext, printed pages 205-211 for chapter 8. Continue from complexes to explicit derived-functor calculations; representation theory chapters are not prerequisites for these algebra sections.

Practice

Resolutions and Ext/Tor problems. Exercise 8.2.2 on existence of projective resolutions; Problem 8.2.5 on independence of resolution; Problem 8.2.7 on finitely generated modules over Z and k[x].. Exercises provided; public solutions not verified. Some problems contain hints.

Try these before moving on

  • For the complex 0 -> Z --6--> Z -> 0 in degrees 1 and 0, compute both homology groups and explain why its differential condition holds.
  • Resolve Z/4Z by free Z-modules and compute Tor_1 over Z of (Z/4Z, Z/6Z) and Ext^1 over Z of (Z/4Z, Z/6Z), showing the kernel or cokernel used in each case.

Already comfortable with this? Skip the complex review if you can construct and check the connecting homomorphism, including independence of representatives. Skip the resolution selection only if you can compute both Ext and Tor in the cyclic example and justify independence of the chosen resolution.
