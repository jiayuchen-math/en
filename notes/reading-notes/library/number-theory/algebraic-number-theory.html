Algebraic Number Theory & Class Field Theory

代数数论：元素分解失效以后怎么办？

独立中文导读 · 非逐句全文翻译 · 修订 2026-10-18

在 Z[−5]\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]Z[−5​] 中，666 有 2⋅32\cdot32⋅3 与 (1+−5)(1−−5)(1+\sqrt{-5})(1-\sqrt{-5})(1+−5​)(1−−5​) 两种本质不同的不可约分解。整数里的唯一分解习惯失效了，问题不是再找一个更聪明的整数算法，而是换一种能保留分解结构的对象。理想由此获得了很具体的用途。

先认清整数环，别急着算类群

推荐 Milne 的 Algebraic Number Theory。第 2 章先学整数环、迹、范数、判别式及整数环的求法，第 3 章接 Dedekind 整环、理想分解与类群，第 4 章再看格与类数有限性的估计。需要域扩张、理想、商与局部化，整性若陌生，可同时补交换代数相应部分。

二次数域的整数环不总是 Z[d]\mathbb Z[\sqrt d]Z[d​]。用 Q(5)\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)Q(5​) 先求真正的整数环、一组整数基和判别式，再计算 (1+5)/2(1+\sqrt5)/2(1+5​)/2 的范数。若这一步尚未分清阶与整数环，直接套后面的素数分解公式容易把前提用错。

把元素与理想分开记账

在 Gaussian 整数中分解理想 (5)(5)(5) 与 (2)(2)(2)，同时记录剩余次数、分歧指数及范数。理想相等、生成元相差单位、元素相等是三件不同的事。类群衡量主理想与一般理想之间的差别，也不能简单解释成“还有几个不唯一分解的元素”。

讲义有章末练习和附录中的部分提示、解答纲要，未逐题核实完整覆盖。第一轮先做二次数域与理想范数的计算，再用一个类数估计组织有限的检查；不必从一开始追一般类域论。

基础稳固后可按问题去局部域、类域论或算术几何。模形式提供另一组算术联系，但需要复分析与自身的定义体系。已经掌握二次例子的读者可以缩短复习，不能因此略过整性或 Dedekind 条件在一般论证中的作用。

Studying Algebraic Number Theory & Class Field Theory

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Unique factorization of elements can fail in number fields, while ideals retain a more stable factorization theory. Milne's introductory notes connect rings of integers, discriminants and Dedekind domains to this change of viewpoint. His class-field notes pursue a later question about abelian extensions. Treat that second work as an advanced continuation requiring local and global foundations, with a substantial distance between the two starting points.

What to work on

  • Compute rings of integers, traces, norms and discriminants in quadratic fields.
  • Factor rational primes into prime ideals with ramification and residue degrees.
  • Distinguish factorization of elements from factorization of ideals.
  • Use ideal classes and a Minkowski bound to organize class-group calculations.

A starting text

Algebraic Number Theory — James S. Milne. Read chapter 2, Rings of Integers, especially norms, traces, discriminants and finding the ring of integers; chapter 3, Dedekind Domains; Factorization, especially ideal factorization, the class group and finding factorizations; then chapter 4, The Finiteness of the Class Number, for lattices and bounds.

Choosing what to read

Find an integral basis in a quadratic field and compute its discriminant, comparing it with the basis you might first guess. Factor a small rational prime into ideals and check the ramification and residue degrees. Distinguish an ideal being principal from an element factoring uniquely. Use a lattice bound to make a class-group calculation finite. Keep localization and valuation examples nearby before beginning a local or global reciprocity argument.

  1. In Z[i], factor the ideals (5) and (2), identify ramification and residue degrees, and justify the factorizations with norms.

    A hint

    Factor the rational integers as Gaussian integers. Show the quotients by the proposed prime generators are fields, rather than inferring primality solely from a drawing.
