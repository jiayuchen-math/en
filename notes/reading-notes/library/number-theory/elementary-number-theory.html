Studying Elementary Number Theory

What to work on

  • Use the Euclidean algorithm and Bezout's identity.
  • Solve congruences and apply the Chinese remainder theorem.
  • Compute modular inverses and powers with stated coprimality conditions.
  • Use quadratic reciprocity and Euler's criterion.

A starting text

Elementary Number Theory: Primes, Congruences, and Secrets — William Stein. Read 1.1 on factorization and the Euclidean algorithm; 2.1-2.3 on congruences, the Chinese remainder theorem, inverses and powers; then 4.1-4.3 on quadratic reciprocity and Euler's criterion.

Practice

Congruence and quadratic-residue exercises with selected hints. Sections 1.3, 2.6 and 4.6 contain the exercise sets. Answers and Hints begins on printed page 149; match the chapter and exercise number.. Public exercises and an Answers and Hints section are present. It contains selected answers and proof hints, not a verified complete worked solution manual.

Try these before moving on

  • Use the extended Euclidean algorithm to find the inverse of 17 modulo 43, and solve x=2 modulo 3, x=3 modulo 5 and x=2 modulo 7, with a uniqueness statement.
  • Determine whether 5 is a quadratic residue modulo 29 using quadratic reciprocity, then find both square roots if they exist.

Already comfortable with this? Skip the congruence review if you can justify the inverse and CRT constructions. Skip the residue selection if reciprocity, its sign and the supplementary laws are usable without a table.
