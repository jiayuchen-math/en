Studying Modular Forms & Arithmetic Geometry

What to work on

  • Check the upper-half-plane action of SL2(Z) and state a modular transformation law.
  • Relate translation invariance to a q-expansion and distinguish holomorphy from meromorphy at a cusp.
  • Compute low-order coefficients of level-one Eisenstein and cusp forms.
  • Apply a Hecke coefficient formula with an explicit normalization.

A starting text

Modular Functions and Modular Forms (Elliptic Modular Curves) — James S. Milne. Read section 1, Preliminaries, and section 2, Elliptic Modular Curves as Riemann Surfaces, for the upper half-plane and quotients. Section 4, Modular Functions and Modular Forms, begins on printed page 48; section 5, Hecke Operators, begins on page 67. Use section 3 on elliptic functions only as needed.

Other ways into the subject
  • Modular Forms: A Computational Approach — William A. Stein, with an appendix by Paul E. Gunnells. Sections 1.1-1.4 for definitions and congruence groups; 2.1-2.4 for level-one forms, the structure theorem, the Miller basis and Hecke operators. Use alongside Milne when explicit q-expansions and algorithms are the priority.

Practice

Level-one modular-form exercises. Section 1.6 Exercises, printed page 11: start with exercise 1.1 on the half-plane action. Section 2.8 Exercises, page 33: exercise 2.3 concerns Eisenstein-series coefficients and 2.8 concerns a Hecke matrix.. Public exercise sets verified in Stein's author-hosted book; public worked solutions were not verified. These exercises are a separate source from Milne's reading notes.

Try these before moving on

  • For gamma=[[a,b],[c,d]] in SL2(R), prove Im(gamma z)=Im(z)/|cz+d| squared when Im(z)>0. Then use -I to show a modular form for SL2(Z) of odd integral weight is zero.
  • With q=exp(2pi i z), E4=1+240 sum_{n>=1} sigma_3(n) q^n and E6=1-504 sum_{n>=1} sigma_5(n) q^n, compute the coefficients through q squared of (E4 cubed-E6 squared)/1728.

Already comfortable with this? Skip the action and definition review if you can prove the first check and explain the cusp condition. Skip coefficient practice if the second check is routine; assess Hecke operators separately through Stein's exercises.
