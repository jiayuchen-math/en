Studying Functional Analysis

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Finite-dimensional linear algebra changes character when completeness, topology and boundedness become part of the problem. Melrose develops Banach and Hilbert spaces alongside the main operator theorems and compact spectral theory. Yosida takes operator semigroups toward evolution equations. The semigroup route answers a more specialized question and should follow, rather than stand in for, the initial normed-space and bounded-operator work.

What to work on

  • Prove completeness or exhibit an incomplete normed space.
  • Compute norms of elementary bounded operators and functionals.
  • Choose between uniform boundedness, open mapping, closed graph and Hahn-Banach arguments.
  • Use orthogonal projections, adjoints and compact-operator spectral theory.

A starting text

Functional Analysis: Lecture Notes for 18.102 — Richard Melrose. Chapter 1, sections 2-6 and 8-12 for Banach spaces and the main operator theorems. Then chapter 3, sections 8-11 for projections and adjoints and 14-18 for compactness, weak convergence and the compact self-adjoint spectral theorem.

Choosing what to read

Test continuity and boundedness on explicit operators, keeping their domains visible. Compare a complete sequence space with an incomplete normed subspace, and identify which proof fails without completeness. When applying an open-mapping or uniform-boundedness theorem, write the hypotheses before the conclusion. In Hilbert space, calculate a projection and its orthogonality relation. Save unbounded generators and evolution problems for the point where dense domains, convergence and operator composition can be handled precisely.

Try these before moving on

Try a proof or calculation before opening the hint. These solutions address the checks below, not the exercises in the linked textbooks.

  1. On C[0,1] with the supremum norm compute the operator norm of L(f)=integral from 0 to 1 of t f(t) dt, proving both an upper bound and attainment.

    A hint

    Bound the absolute integral using the supremum norm. A constant function can attain the bound because the weight is nonnegative.

    Work through the solution

    If this was the sticking point:

Already comfortable with this? Skip Banach-space review if completeness and boundedness proofs are routine. Skip the Hilbert-space selection only if you can complete the compact-operator check and distinguish spectrum from point spectrum; finite-dimensional eigenvalue calculations alone do not suffice.
