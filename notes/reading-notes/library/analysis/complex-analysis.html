Studying Complex Analysis & Several Complex Variables

What to work on

  • Use complex differentiability and Cauchy-Riemann equations with the required regularity hypotheses.
  • Apply Cauchy's formula to obtain derivatives and estimates.
  • Classify isolated singularities and compute residues.
  • Compare zero sets and holomorphic extension in one and several complex variables.

A starting text

Guide to Cultivating Complex Analysis: Working the Complex Field — Jiří Lebl. Read 2.1 on holomorphic functions, 3.2-3.3 on Cauchy's theorem and consequences, 4.4 on Laurent series, and 5.1-5.4 on zeros, singularities and residues. Review line integrals in 3.1 if needed.

Other ways into the subject
  • Tasty Bits of Several Complex Variables: A Whirlwind Tour of the Subject — Jiří Lebl. Sections 1.1-1.3 on functions, power series and derivatives; 1.6 on extension and zero sets; 2.1 on domains of holomorphy and holomorphic extension. Continue after one-variable Cauchy theory to see which familiar geometric conclusions change in several variables.

Practice

Holomorphic-function and residue exercises. Exercises 2.1.1-2.1.6 for differentiability and Cauchy-Riemann; exercises embedded in sections 4.4 and 5.3 for Laurent series and residues.. Exercises provided; public solutions not verified.

Try these before moving on

  • Determine every point where f(z) = absolute value of z squared is complex differentiable, using the derivative definition as well as the Cauchy-Riemann equations.
  • Compute the positively oriented unit-circle integral of exp(z)/z squared and identify the residue.
  • In C squared describe the zero set of f(z,w)=zw and show that its zeros are not isolated. Explain why this does not contradict the one-variable isolated-zero theorem.

Already comfortable with this? Skip the one-variable selection if you can do the first two checks and state the domain hypotheses of Cauchy's theorem. Take the several-variable route only after that; skip it if extension phenomena and positive-dimensional zero sets are already familiar.
