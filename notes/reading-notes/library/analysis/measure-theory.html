Studying Measure & Integration

Start here · Choose a text · Practice · Check your understanding · Continue · Full reading list

Measure theory separates the size of sets from the integration of functions built on them. Axler organizes a route through measurable spaces, measures, integrals and convergence before iterated integration. Tao offers a second development of Lebesgue measure and integration that can be useful when you want to revisit how the objects are constructed. Choose one main exposition and use the other to investigate a specific proof or example.

What to work on

  • Check measurability through inverse images.
  • Construct integrals from nonnegative simple functions.
  • Choose an applicable convergence theorem and verify its hypotheses.
  • Distinguish Tonelli's nonnegativity condition from Fubini's integrability condition.

A starting text

Measure, Integration & Real Analysis — Sheldon Axler. Read 2B-2C on measurable spaces and measures, 3A-3B on integration and convergence, then 5B on iterated integrals. Consult 2D only when the construction of Lebesgue measure is the gap.

Choosing what to read

Check measurability before writing an integral, and distinguish a nonnegative function from an integrable signed function. For each limit interchange, identify the applicable theorem and verify every hypothesis on the actual sequence. Compare increasing indicator functions with moving spikes to see why different convergence assumptions matter. Before swapping integration order, decide whether Tonelli or Fubini applies. Write the underlying measures and product space explicitly; similar-looking integral formulas can have different justifications.
