Studying Fourier & Harmonic Analysis

What to work on

  • Compute Fourier coefficients with a consistent normalization.
  • Distinguish pointwise and L2 convergence of Fourier series.
  • Use convolution to relate Fourier coefficients or transforms.
  • Apply Fourier inversion and the L2 extension under their stated hypotheses.

A starting text

Measure, Integration & Real Analysis - Fourier Analysis chapter — Sheldon Axler. Chapter 11: 11A Fourier Series and Poisson Integral, 11B Fourier Series and Lp of Unit Circle, and 11C Fourier Transform, printed pages 340-379. Review orthonormal bases in 8C only as needed.

Other ways into the subject
  • EE261: The Fourier Transform and its Applications — Brad Osgood. Read the linked HTML/PDF transcripts for lectures 3-4 on Fourier-series convergence and orthogonality, then 6-9 on transforms, Gaussian examples, shifting, scaling and convolution. Use the engineering examples and public problem sets to connect the analytic statements with explicit calculations.

Practice

Stanford EE261 problem sets and solutions. Assignments table: start with Problem Sets 1-3 alongside lectures 3-9; match later sets to their lecture due markers.. The public assignments table lists question and solution links for problem sets 1-9 and exams. The 16-page Problem Set 1 solution PDF was opened; the remaining solution PDFs were not individually inspected, and complete exercise coverage is not claimed.

Try these before moving on

  • For the 2pi-periodic extension of f(x)=x on (-pi,pi), compute its sine-series coefficients and identify the Fourier-series limit at a jump.
  • Using F(f)(xi)=integral of f(x) exp(-2pi i x xi) dx, compute the transform of the indicator of [-1/2,1/2] and then the transform of its convolution with itself.

Already comfortable with this? Skip basic Fourier calculations if both checks are routine. Skip the analytic reading only if you can also justify an L2 convergence statement and specify hypotheses for inversion; an informal transform table does not settle those questions.
