Studying Dynamical Systems & Ergodic Theory

What to work on

  • Describe a flow, its orbits and invariant sets.
  • Distinguish stability, attraction and asymptotic stability.
  • Use a Lyapunov function and state what its derivative proves.
  • Determine local stability of fixed points of a discrete map.

A starting text

Ordinary Differential Equations and Dynamical Systems — Gerald Teschl. Read 6.1-6.3 on flows, orbits and invariant sets and 6.5-6.6 on stability and Lyapunov methods; use 9.1 for linear stability. For a discrete route read 10.1-10.2 and 10.4 on logistic maps, periodic points and local behavior.

Practice

Flows, stability and discrete-dynamics problems. In chapter 6 start with Problems 6.8-6.10 on limits, periodicity and nonwandering points. Then use problems embedded in 6.5-6.6 and chapter 10 alongside the corresponding readings.. Public numbered problems verified within the author PDF, some with hints; a public worked solution collection was not verified.

Try these before moving on

  • For x'=-x and y'=-2y, write the flow and use V=x squared+y squared to prove global asymptotic stability of the origin. Explain why V=x squared+y squared cannot prove attraction for x'=-y, y'=x.
  • For F_r(x)=r x(1-x) on [0,1] with 0<r<=4, find its fixed points in [0,1]. Determine the parameter ranges where the derivative test proves local attraction or repulsion; identify the parameters where that test is inconclusive.

Already comfortable with this? Skip flow and stability review if you can prove the first check with the definitions of stability and attraction. Skip the discrete selection if you can give the derivative argument and recognize its inconclusive boundary cases. Ergodic theory needs a separate measure-based route.
